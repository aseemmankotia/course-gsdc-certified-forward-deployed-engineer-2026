## Chapter 10 Cheat Sheet — Client Discovery, Scoping & Business Case Development

### Stakeholder Mapping (Power/Interest Grid)
| Power | Interest | Quadrant | Engagement Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | High | Manage Closely | Frequent, detailed updates |
| High | Low | Keep Satisfied | Periodic, high-level updates |
| Low | High | Keep Informed | Regular, detailed updates |
| Low | Low | Monitor | Minimal, periodic checks |

**Exam trap:** Distractors often swap Manage Closely and Keep Satisfied strategies — remember power alone doesn't warrant heavy detail; interest does.

### Discovery Methodology Comparison
| Methodology | Focus | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| BANT | Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline | Quick, simple qualification |
| MEDDIC | Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria/process, Pain, Champion | Complex, multi-stakeholder enterprise deals |
| SPIN | Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff | Consultative questioning during discovery calls |

**Exam trap:** MEDDIC is chosen when the question emphasizes economic buyer + champion + decision criteria specifically — not just 'qualify the deal' generically (that's BANT).

### Business Case & ROI
- **ROI (%) = (Net Benefit / Cost) × 100**
- **Payback Period = Total Cost / Periodic Savings**
- Hard benefits (quantified $) vs. Soft benefits (qualitative, e.g., morale, agility) — keep them **separate**, never blend soft into hard ROI totals.
- Always show cost, benefit, and timeframe together — a business case without timeframe is incomplete.

### Technical Writing for Non-Technical Audiences
- **Lead with outcome, not method.** First 1–2 sentences = business impact + cost.
- Subordinate technical detail — use appendices or supporting sections, not the opening.
- Avoid jargon; if a term needs an acronym expansion, it likely needs a plain-language substitute instead.

### Cross-Layer Communication (Exec / Business / Technical)
| Layer | Cares About | Content Depth |
|---|---|---|
| Executive (CFO/CEO) | Cost, ROI, risk, timeline | High-level, numbers-driven |
| Business (VP Ops) | Process impact, adoption, efficiency | Medium, outcome-focused |
| Technical (CTO/Architects) | Architecture, feasibility, integration | Deep, detailed |

**Structure rule:** Shared outcome framing up front → audience-specific sections after, so each stakeholder can 'tune in' to their relevant depth.

### Scoping Workshop Agenda (2-hour template)
1. Business goals & success criteria — 30 min
2. Current-state / pain points — 30 min
3. Future-state options & trade-offs — 45 min
4. Next steps & owners — 15 min

**Exam trap:** Never front-load deep technical architecture before establishing business goals — this is a commonly tested wrong-answer pattern.
