## Chapter 12: Full Exam Simulation and Test-Taking Strategy — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Total exam time and question count for this certification's format? | 90 minutes, 40 questions. Exam tip: that's ~2.25 min/question on average, but budget only 1.5 min/question on your first pass to build a review buffer. |
| 2 | If you spend 1.5 minutes/question on a first pass through all 40 questions, how much buffer time remains? | ~30 minutes (60 min used of the 90 min total). Exam tip: reserve this buffer exclusively for flagged questions. |
| 3 | How large should your flagged-question list ideally be at the end of a first pass? | Small — reserve flags only for genuine two-option toss-ups. Exam tip: over-flagging leaves too many items for your review buffer to cover. |
| 4 | Flag-and-move vs. dwell-and-solve — which wins on a timed exam? | Flag-and-move. Exam tip: always select a provisional best guess before flagging, so a blank answer is never left if time runs out. |
| 5 | First-pass review vs. second-pass review — what's the difference in purpose? | First pass: answer every question at a steady pace, flagging uncertain ones. Second pass: spend the leftover buffer only on flagged items. Exam tip: don't reread confidently-answered questions unless time remains after flags are cleared. |
| 6 | Correct procedure when you hit an ambiguous scenario question mid-exam? | 1) Eliminate obviously wrong options. 2) Select your best remaining guess. 3) Flag it. 4) Move to the next question immediately. Exam tip: never leave a flagged question blank. |
| 7 | Correct procedure when reviewing flagged questions in your buffer time? | 1) Open only flagged items (skip answered ones). 2) Reread the full scenario, not just the options. 3) Re-eliminate distractors. 4) Change the answer only if you find a concrete reason. Exam tip: don't change an answer on a pure hunch. |
| 8 | What does a domain-by-domain score review reveal that an overall score does not? | It isolates which specific domain(s) are weak even when the overall average looks passing. Exam tip: a strong overall score can still hide one critically weak domain — review by domain, not just in aggregate. |
| 9 | Most common recurring trap pattern across all exam domains? | A distractor option that is technically correct but fails to satisfy every constraint stated in the scenario. Exam tip: always re-check the option against ALL stated requirements, not just the most obvious one. |
| 10 | Best strategic use of a complimentary retake attached to your exam registration? | Treat it strictly as a safety net for an unlucky first attempt after full preparation — not as a reason to under-prepare or as a substitute for practice simulations. Exam tip: your first attempt should always be your best-prepared attempt. |
| 11 | When 25 minutes remain and all flagged questions are cleared, what's the best use of leftover time? | A light final scan of confidently-answered questions for careless errors (e.g., misclicks). Exam tip: don't second-guess confident answers without a concrete reason — this often introduces new errors. |
| 12 | Purpose of a full 40-question simulation before the real attempt? | Builds timing endurance and surfaces domain-specific weaknesses under realistic time pressure. Exam tip: simulations are diagnostic tools, not a preview of exact real-exam content — don't memorize questions, learn the reasoning patterns. |
