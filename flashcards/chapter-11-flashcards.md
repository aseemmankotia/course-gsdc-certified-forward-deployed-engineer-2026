## Chapter 11: PoC-to-Production, Demo Delivery, and Trusted-Advisor Engagement — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | PoC vs MVP vs Production — key differentiator | PoC proves technical feasibility (short-lived, throwaway). MVP proves business value with real users on real data. Production requires SLAs, monitoring, security review, and a named business owner. Exam tip: 'production-ready' always implies operational rigor, not just accuracy. |
| 2 | Problem-Insight-Solution-Impact arc — 4 stages | 1) Problem (client's pain in their words) 2) Insight (why current approaches fail) 3) Solution (mapped directly to the insight) 4) Impact (quantified outcome). Exam tip: Impact always closes the arc, right before Q&A. |
| 3 | Live demo fails mid-presentation — best practice response | Briefly acknowledge, pivot to a pre-recorded/backup output, keep narrating the insight. Exam trap: restarting live in front of stakeholders wastes trust and time. |
| 4 | Trusted-advisor engagement vs project-based SOW | Trusted-advisor = retainer, recurring cadence (e.g., quarterly business reviews), tied to KPIs, ongoing relationship. Project SOW = fixed scope, ends at delivery. Exam tip: choose trusted-advisor when the client wants ongoing strategic guidance. |
| 5 | When to trigger an Executive Business Review (EBR) proactively | Before renewal (commonly ~60-90 days out) OR as soon as usage/adoption metrics go flat or decline. Exam tip: never wait until the renewal call to raise a flat-usage signal. |
| 6 | Crisis management protocol — first step | Acknowledge transparently, contain impact, and establish a communication cadence with the client — root cause investigation runs in parallel, not before contact. Exam trap: 'wait for full RCA before contacting client' is always wrong. |
| 7 | PoC succeeds technically but production stalls — most common blocker | Lack of organizational buy-in / no change champion, not a technical failure. Fix: identify a champion, run a phased pilot with defined success metrics. Exam tip: adoption problems are people problems, not model problems. |
| 8 | Success metrics for a renewal motion — what to anchor them to | The original success criteria defined at PoC/MVP kickoff, not vanity metrics. Renewal conversations must show measurable progress against those original KPIs. Exam tip: renewal readiness = KPI traceability from kickoff to now. |
| 9 | Handling a tough, skeptical question during a live AI demo | Validate the concern, point to the specific output/metric that addresses it, and offer a detailed follow-up if it's outside scope. Exam trap: overpromising on the spot to avoid an awkward silence. |
| 10 | Post-demo follow-up — mandatory elements | Recap of the problem-insight-solution-impact arc, written answers to open questions raised live, and a next step with a specific date and owner. Exam tip: a demo without a dated follow-up action is considered incomplete. |
| 11 | PoC-to-Production transition — the 4 gates | 1) Defined SLAs 2) Monitoring/observability in place 3) Security/compliance review passed 4) Named business owner accountable for ongoing metrics. Exam tip: accuracy alone never satisfies the production gate. |
| 12 | Visualizing AI outputs in a demo — best practice | Show outputs inside the client's own business context/dashboard, not raw JSON or model logs, so non-technical stakeholders see impact, not internals. Exam tip: raw model output in front of execs is a common credibility-losing mistake. |
