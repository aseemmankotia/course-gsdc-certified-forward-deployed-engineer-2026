## Chapter 11: PoC-to-Production, Demo Delivery, and Trusted-Advisor Engagement — Practice Questions

**Q1.** A PoC has proven technically sound, but the client's operations team is quietly resisting the move to production, causing the initiative to stall. Per change-management best practice for PoC-to-Production transitions, what should the delivery team do FIRST?

A) Rebuild the PoC with more advanced features to win over skeptics
B) Identify a change champion within the ops team and run a phased pilot with defined success metrics before full cutover
C) Escalate directly to the client's executive sponsor and demand a hard deadline
D) Wait for the client organization to become ready on its own timeline

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Production stalls are almost always adoption/people problems, not technical ones. A change champion plus a phased pilot with measurable checkpoints builds internal trust and de-risks the cutover.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- More features doesn't address the real blocker, which is organizational buy-in, not capability
- Escalating to force a deadline burns trust with the team that will operate the system daily
- Passive waiting has no defined success criteria and risks the deal going cold indefinitely

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q2.** During a live client demo, the model API times out in front of key stakeholders. What is the BEST immediate response?

A) Briefly acknowledge the issue, pivot to a pre-captured backup output, and continue narrating the same insight
B) Restart the laptop and reload everything from scratch while apologizing repeatedly
C) End the demo early and ask to reschedule for another day
D) Blame the network connection and keep attempting to fix it live for several minutes

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

A brief acknowledgment plus a pre-planned fallback keeps the narrative moving and prevents the failure from becoming the story — this is the core skill of 'handling live demos under uncertainty.'

**Why the others are wrong:**
- A full restart wastes stakeholder time and signals lack of preparation
- Ending early forfeits the opportunity and momentum entirely
- Live troubleshooting in front of executives erodes confidence and derails the narrative arc

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q3.** Which set of criteria correctly gates a move from MVP to full Production, rather than staying at MVP?

A) The model achieves greater than 90% accuracy in the development environment
B) An executive sponsor has personally viewed a live demo of the system
C) The MVP has run for 30 consecutive days without a crash
D) Defined SLAs, monitoring/observability, a completed security review, and a named business owner accountable for ongoing metrics

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Production readiness is an operational bar — SLAs, observability, security sign-off, and clear ownership — not a purely technical or uptime metric.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Accuracy in dev says nothing about operational readiness or ownership
- Executive visibility builds sponsorship but is not an operational gate
- Uptime alone ignores security, monitoring, and accountability requirements

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q4.** In the problem-insight-solution-impact demo narrative arc, what is the primary purpose of the 'insight' stage?

A) To showcase the technical architecture in detail
B) To bridge from the stated problem to why current approaches fail, creating the need for the proposed solution
C) To present pricing tiers and licensing options
D) To recap the meeting agenda before starting

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Insight is the pivot point: it explains root cause or why existing methods fall short, which makes the subsequent solution feel necessary rather than arbitrary.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Architecture detail belongs later or in a technical appendix, not the insight stage
- Pricing belongs to a separate commercial conversation, not the narrative core
- An agenda recap is filler and adds no narrative value

</details>

**Q5.** Where should the quantified business impact (ROI, hours saved, cost avoided) be placed within the problem-insight-solution-impact demo structure?

A) At the very beginning, before the problem is even stated
B) Only in a follow-up email sent after the meeting
C) Immediately after the solution is shown, closing the arc right before opening the floor for questions
D) Scattered throughout the demo wherever it feels natural

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Impact is the payoff of the arc — it must land right after the solution to give stakeholders a concrete 'so what' before discussion begins.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Leading with impact before the problem removes the context needed to make the number meaningful
- Deferring impact entirely to email loses the moment of maximum stakeholder attention
- Scattering impact dilutes its punch and breaks the narrative's logical flow

</details>

**Q6.** A client's contract renews in 60 days. Usage metrics have been flat for a quarter and there has been no executive sponsor engagement recently. What should the account team do?

A) Proactively schedule an executive business review now, tying usage data back to original success metrics and proposing an expansion or remediation plan
B) Wait until the renewal call to raise the usage concern
C) Immediately offer a discount to secure the renewal
D) Escalate the account to legal for contract review

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Flat usage plus disengaged sponsorship is an early renewal-risk signal; the trusted-advisor motion is to proactively re-engage with data tied to original KPIs well before the renewal deadline.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Waiting until the renewal call removes any runway to fix the underlying adoption issue
- Discounting without diagnosing the adoption problem treats a symptom, not the cause
- Legal escalation is premature and unrelated to an adoption/engagement issue

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q7.** A client wants ongoing strategic guidance and quarterly roadmap alignment rather than a single deliverable. Which engagement model BEST fits this need?

A) A retainer-based trusted-advisor engagement with a recurring cadence, such as quarterly business reviews tied to KPIs
B) A fixed-scope statement of work with no touchpoints after final delivery
C) A repeated PoC re-run every quarter with no production commitment
D) A support-ticket-only relationship triggered only when something breaks

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Ongoing strategic guidance requires a recurring, KPI-linked cadence — the defining feature of a trusted-advisor retainer model.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- A fixed-scope SOW ends at delivery and has no mechanism for ongoing alignment
- Re-running PoCs indefinitely avoids production value and provides no strategic cadence
- Ticket-only support is reactive and offers no proactive roadmap alignment

</details>

**Q8.** A client experiences a costly business error caused by a model's output. What is the FIRST step in a proper crisis management protocol?

A) Acknowledge the issue transparently, contain the impact, and establish a communication cadence with the client while root cause investigation begins
B) Immediately assign internal blame to prevent recurrence
C) Wait until the full root cause analysis is complete before contacting the client
D) Offer a refund immediately without any investigation

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Crisis protocols prioritize transparent, timely communication and impact containment in parallel with — not after — root cause work, preserving trust during uncertainty.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Internal blame-assignment before communication delays client transparency and damages trust
- Silence until full RCA leaves the client uninformed during active impact, which is the most commonly missed trap
- A refund without investigation is a premature commercial reaction, not a crisis containment step

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>
